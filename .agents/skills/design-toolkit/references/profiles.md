# Curated design profiles

These are original shorthand profiles for translating a reference into a new product. They describe tendencies, not exact copies.

## Linear / linear.app
Use for technical SaaS, developer tools, issue trackers, and products that benefit from quiet precision. Favor near-black layered surfaces, very restrained lavender/blue accents, thin separators, compact spacing, sharp hierarchy, and product UI as the main visual evidence. Typography should feel dense and engineered rather than oversized and promotional. Avoid ambient gradients and decorative cards.

## Apple
Use for product-led storytelling, hardware-like polish, consumer software, or premium simplicity. Let imagery or the product itself dominate, keep chrome quiet, use generous whitespace, high-quality typography, and a single disciplined interactive blue. Alternate light and dark sections only when the content benefits from a cinematic shift. Avoid excessive borders, badges, and decorative gradients.

## Figma
Use for creative tools and collaborative products that can carry more personality. Start with a rigorous black/white editorial frame, then introduce large flat color fields or playful blocks as deliberate interruptions. Use simple geometry, direct copy, and expressive composition rather than glossy effects.

## Notion
Use for flexible workspaces, knowledge products, and products with many content types. Favor warm neutrals, friendly type, generous content examples, soft illustrative color, and clear modular grouping. The page can feel playful without becoming noisy. Avoid turning every module into an isolated card; let sections read like a workspace.

## Stripe
Use for financial infrastructure, APIs, and products that need authority without looking bureaucratic. Pair deep navy ink with a concentrated indigo accent, editorial display type, careful numeric treatment, and clean near-white surfaces. Atmospheric color can appear in a controlled hero region, but the product and documentation should quickly become precise and structured.

## Airbnb
Use for marketplaces, travel, local services, and warm consumer products. Build around photography, white space, approachable typography, friendly rounded search/action controls, and one energetic accent color. The interface should feel human and tactile rather than tech-demo-like.

## Vercel
Use for developer platforms and products that want stark technical clarity. Favor black/white contrast, strict grids, monospaced secondary information, thin borders, and minimal decoration. Let diagrams, code, and product behavior create interest. Avoid soft lifestyle styling.

## Raycast
Use for productivity tools, command surfaces, and power-user software. Dark surfaces, compact density, clear keyboard affordances, crisp icons, and controlled luminous accents work well. Keep interactions fast and direct; avoid marketing-page ornament inside functional UI.

## Spotify
Use for media, entertainment, and discovery. Strong imagery, dark foundations, bold content groupings, and a vivid accent can work, but hierarchy should come from content and artwork rather than a sea of colored cards. Prioritize browsing rhythm and immediate playback/action affordances.

## Shopify
Use for commerce and business tooling that needs to feel approachable but capable. Favor warm/neutral foundations, clear content hierarchy, useful illustration or product examples, and restrained green accents. Balance friendly brand expression with dense operational UI.

## Supabase
Use for developer infrastructure that wants a more approachable open-source feel. Dark neutral surfaces, a focused green accent, code/data examples, and modular product UI are effective. Keep the grid disciplined and avoid turning the green into a glow effect everywhere.

## Slack
Use for collaboration products that need warmth and recognizability. Combine strong information hierarchy with a limited set of expressive colors. Functional surfaces should stay calm; save brand energy for onboarding, marketing, or moments of emphasis.
